About

STATUS: PUBLISHEDDATE: 2026-07-23

I’m Elliot Chrystal, a computer science student at the University of Alberta with a minor in philosophy. I work as a software developer and use this site as a place to collect projects, technical notes, and the kinds of experiments that are worth finishing even when they begin as small curiosities.

I like building software that is clear, direct, and useful. A lot of that instinct comes from moving between different kinds of work: university courses, paid development, and personal projects that exist mostly because I wanted to understand something more deeply.

Outside of programming, I spend time on film photography, vinyl records, audiovisual hardware, and jazz trumpet. Those interests pull me toward tools and systems with texture and constraint, which is probably part of why I appreciate both modern software and older, more durable ways of doing things.

Background

Computer science keeps me learning quickly. Philosophy keeps me honest about how I think. Between the two, I’ve ended up with a strong bias toward systems that are understandable, stable, and built with intent.

I’m interested in new tools when they solve a real problem, but I don’t think novelty is enough on its own. In practice, I care more about whether something is reliable, legible, and worth maintaining.
